# 02 — Optimisation Algorithms for 2D Cutting and Nesting

## 1. Introduction

Two-dimensional cutting optimisation involves arranging required components within available sheets or plates while minimising waste and satisfying manufacturing constraints.

Different algorithmic approaches can be used to solve these problems. This section reviews four approaches identified in the GoSmarter research brief:

1. Heuristic algorithms
2. Metaheuristic algorithms
3. Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP)
4. Constraint Programming (CP)

The objective is to understand their strengths, limitations and potential applications in metals manufacturing.

## 2. Heuristic Algorithms

Heuristics are practical methods designed to find good solutions without necessarily evaluating every possible arrangement.

For example, a simple heuristic may arrange larger rectangular components first, then place smaller components within the remaining available spaces.

Common rectangular packing approaches include:

- **MaxRects:** Tracks available rectangular spaces and uses placement rules to select positions.
- **Skyline:** Represents the available packing boundary as a changing skyline.
- **Guillotine:** Uses strategies based on dividing rectangular regions.

These algorithm families are implemented in the open-source Rectpack library.

### Strengths

- Can produce useful arrangements relatively quickly.
- Suitable for establishing rectangular nesting baselines.
- Often simpler to implement than complex mathematical optimisation models.

### Limitations

- Do not generally guarantee globally optimal solutions.
- Solution quality depends on the selected heuristic and input data.
- Additional functionality may be required for metals-specific constraints.

### Potential GoSmarter Application

Heuristic algorithms could provide fast initial rectangular cutting layouts for sheet and plate materials.

## 3. Metaheuristic Algorithms

Metaheuristics are broader search strategies used to explore and potentially improve candidate solutions.

Examples include:

- **Genetic Algorithms:** Explore candidate solutions using processes inspired by evolutionary selection and variation.
- **Simulated Annealing:** Explores alternative solutions and may temporarily accept worse solutions to escape local optima.
- **Tabu Search:** Uses search memory to discourage repeated or unproductive moves.

### Strengths

- Can explore large and complex solution spaces.
- May improve arrangements generated by simpler heuristics.
- Can be adapted to different optimisation objectives.

### Limitations

- Usually do not guarantee global optimality.
- May require careful parameter tuning.
- Computational runtime and solution quality can vary.

### Potential GoSmarter Application

Metaheuristics could be investigated for improving initial nesting arrangements, particularly where the number of possible layouts is large.

## 4. Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP)

Mixed-Integer Programming formulates optimisation problems using mathematical decision variables, constraints and an objective function.

Some variables must take integer values, while others may be continuous.

For example, a binary variable could represent whether a particular steel plate is selected:

- 1 = Plate selected
- 0 = Plate not selected

A model could then attempt to minimise material cost subject to stock availability and other requirements.

### Strengths

- Provides a structured mathematical framework for optimisation.
- Can model stock selection, costs and resource allocation.
- Suitable solvers can sometimes prove optimality or provide bounds on solution quality.

### Limitations

- Complex formulations may require significant computational resources.
- Geometric nesting constraints can make models harder to solve.
- Developing an accurate industrial model requires careful mathematical formulation.

### Potential GoSmarter Application

MIP could support decisions about which certified steel plates or reusable offcuts should be allocated to production orders.

However, selecting sufficient material area does not by itself guarantee that required shapes can be geometrically nested.

## 5. Constraint Programming (CP)

Constraint Programming searches for solutions that satisfy specified rules governing decision variables.

Examples of relevant manufacturing constraints include:

- Required parts must not overlap.
- Parts must fit within available stock.
- Materials must meet specified requirements.
- Machine restrictions must be respected.
- Production activities may need to follow scheduling rules.

Google OR-Tools provides CP-SAT, a constraint-based optimisation solver.

### Strengths

- Supports complex logical and combinatorial constraints.
- Useful for scheduling and resource-allocation problems.
- Can model rectangular non-overlap and other geometric constraints.

### Limitations

- Requires a suitable problem formulation.
- Complex instances may be computationally demanding.
- Irregular geometric nesting may require substantial additional modelling or specialised tools.

### Potential GoSmarter Application

Constraint programming could support manufacturing planning involving rectangular placement, material allocation and production scheduling.

## 6. Comparison of Approaches

| Approach | Potential Strength | Main Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Heuristics | Fast generation of practical layouts | No general optimality guarantee |
| Metaheuristics | Flexible improvement and exploration | Performance depends on configuration |
| MIP | Mathematical optimisation of allocation and cost decisions | Complex models may be slow |
| CP | Flexible modelling of logical and scheduling constraints | Requires careful formulation |

These approaches are not mutually exclusive. Some optimisation systems combine several methods.

## 7. Preliminary Research Assessment

For GoSmarter, a combination of optimisation approaches may be worth investigating.

For example:

1. Filter available steel stock according to mandatory material requirements.
2. Use a nesting heuristic to generate initial cutting layouts.
3. Explore mathematical optimisation methods for stock selection and production constraints.
4. Evaluate alternative plans using material utilisation, cost, computation time and manufacturing feasibility.

This is a proposed research direction, not a validated architecture or a description of GoSmarter's current implementation.

Further investigation and benchmarking are required before recommending a production approach.
